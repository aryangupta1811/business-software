## D4: entry log ID and time set by the database only

**Question:** should `entry_log`'s `id` and `created_at` always come from the database, so the app can't choose them? (PROGRESS P1.4 note (e).)

**Today:** the app's database role, `app_runtime`, can insert a log row with any `id` and any `created_at`. It can't change or delete a row afterwards (ADR 0028), but a bug, or anyone holding the app's database password, could write a row that looks as if it happened last month. Because the ID is a UUIDv7, which carries a time inside it, the ID can be backdated too, and the viewer orders by ID (P2.6).

**Recommended: yes.** Only the database sets both.

**Why:** the log's value is that history can't be rewritten. With D4, "the app can't edit, delete or backdate history: the database stamps the time" is a stronger rule, and a stronger interview answer, than "the app can't edit or delete it". It doesn't make every row true: the app can still insert a wrong row (a wrong actor, a made-up action), only with the real time on it.

**The price:** demo data (P1.20 (c)) is generated in one go, through the app's own services (ADR 0016). Bills can carry a business date from a clock the seed sets, but every log row will show the **day the seed ran**. So in the demo shop:
- months of bills have log rows all dated the same day, and the viewer says so in one line ("demo history was generated on <date>");
- flag rule 2, out-of-hours activity (06 Q3), only fires on what visitors do live;
- flag rules 1 and 3 (cancellations, write-downs) would see months of cancellations and write-downs on one day and fire for the seed day. **Recommended fix:** rules 1 and 3 count by the action's business date, which each row carries in its `after` values (the bill's date, the adjustment's date), not by `created_at`. Live, the two dates are the same day; for the seed, the flags spread over the months the seed pretends. (The other fix, skipping the seed run's rows in flag rules, leaves the demo with no flags in its history.)

**Options considered:**
- **A. Database only, by column grants (recommended):** as above.
- **B. Keep today's rule:** the seed can backdate realistically, but so can a bug.
- **C. Database only, plus a separate seed-only role** that may set the time on demo databases: realistic history and strict rules for the app, but one more role and an exception to explain. The live demo is the only deployed database, so the exception would sit on the live server.
- **D. A `BEFORE INSERT` trigger that overwrites `id` and `created_at`:** plain Drizzle inserts keep working unchanged (no gotcha below). But it is hidden logic: a bug that passes a time gets no error, the value is silently replaced, and the guard test must check that the trigger exists and is enabled, because a disabled trigger quietly brings back today's rule. A refused insert (A) is easier to see and to test.

**Effect on P1.4 (Day 3), about an hour more:**
- A migration that removes `app_runtime`'s INSERT on the table and grants INSERT only on the other columns (column-level grants).
- A test that inserting `id` or `created_at` fails with `42501`, and a guard rule (`src/tenancy-guard.test.ts`) that `app_runtime` has no INSERT on those two columns.
- A dated note on ADR 0028, and P1.20 (c) updated.
- **Gotcha to handle:** Drizzle 0.45 names every column in an INSERT and sends `default` for the ones you leave out (checked in its `pg-core/dialect.js`). PostgreSQL checks the INSERT right for every column named, so after the grant change a plain Drizzle insert would probably be refused. `recordEntry` would then write its insert with an explicit column list. P1.4 proves this with the test first.

### 07 Q4: what time is the daily summary sent?

**Recommended:** at a time the owner sets, **21:30 IST** by default (before quiet hours start). It covers **everything since the last summary**, so a sale at 21:45 (shop hours run to 22:00) appears in the next one instead of in none.

**Why:** most shops are winding down by then; one message a day is easy to read.

**Gap this raises, the server being off:** the demo server and database are stopped outside work sessions and every night at 23:30 IST (stage 5). A scheduled job can't run on a stopped server. **Recommended:** at most **one summary per business per day**, recorded so that sending is idempotent (from stage 6, every merge restarts the worker, and a restart must never send a second copy). When the worker starts, it sends **the latest missed summary**, marked "late"; older missed ones are skipped (the next summary still covers everything since the last one sent). **Effect:** a catch-up check in P2.7/P2.8 with tests for a restart and for several missed days.

**Effect on the schedule:** the summary's lines come from tasks built at different times: sales and payment modes (P1.14, Day 5, a parallel lane), absent staff (P2.3, Day 5, a parallel lane), udhaar collected (P1.18, Day 6), expiring batches (P1.10, Day 6). ### 06 Q1: are the owner's own actions logged?

**Recommended:** yes. Close it as answered by `00` #20 ("every action is recorded against the person logged in"). The viewer can hide the owner's own entries with a filter.

**Why:** already decided; a log with gaps can't be trusted. **Effect:** none.

### 06 Q2: how long are logs kept?

**Recommended:** forever, as ADR 0028 has it today.

**Why:** GST law asks a business to keep its accounts for about six years (72 months after the annual return is due), and the log shows who changed those records and when. Size isn't a problem: a busy shop writing 500 entries a day at roughly 1 KB each adds about 180 MB a year, against RDS's 20 GB.

**The trade-off:** the log's old → new values include personal data (customers' and staff members' names and phone numbers). India's privacy law (the DPDP Act) expects personal data to be kept only as long as it's needed, so "forever" for the business record doesn't mean forever for a person's details in it.

**Effect:** none now. A future privacy request anonymises personal fields and keeps the records (ADR 0007, Consequences).

### 06 Q3: which patterns count as "unusual", and who sees the flags?

**Recommended:** the owner sees flags: in the viewer, on the owner dashboard (`08`) and as a count in the daily summary (07 A3). For staff, seeing flags is a feature switch that starts **off** (`00` #17). Four rules in v1, each with a number the owner can change:

1. **Many cancellations:** one person cancels 3 or more bills in a day.
2. **Out-of-hours activity:** any change made outside shop hours (owner sets them; 08:00–22:00 by default).
3. **Big stock write-down:** one person's stock adjustments that reduce stock are worth more than ₹1,000 at purchase price in a day. The value at purchase price must be stored in the `stock.adjusted` row when it's written; otherwise re-checking an old day would use today's price.
4. **Price changed back:** an item's price is lowered and raised back within the same day (the classic "cheap sale to a friend"). *Optional; adds about half a session to P2.6.*

**Why:** simple counts the owner understands beat clever rules nobody can explain. Flags are worked out from the log when they're shown, not stored, so changing a number re-checks old days too.

**Effect:** P2.6 (Day 5) includes rules 1–3, or 1–4; P2.9 (Day 7) shows the count on the dashboard. With D4 "yes": rule 2 only fires on live activity in the demo, and rules 1 and 3 count by the action's business date, not `created_at` (see D4, "The price").

### 05 Q1: who marks attendance?

**Recommended:** **the owner**, or a staff member the owner switches "mark attendance" on for, on one daily register screen for the whole branch. Everyone starts as present; tap to change. Staff don't mark their own.

**Why:** the counter computer is shared, so self-marking is easy to fake; one person marking everyone takes under a minute. Changing a past day needs a reason and goes in the entry log.

**Effect:** P2.3 (Day 5) builds one register screen.

### 05 Q2: how are half-days, late arrivals and overtime counted in pay?

**Recommended:**
- **Monthly salary:** one day's pay = monthly salary ÷ the number of days in that month (28 to 31). Weekly offs, shop holidays and paid leaves are paid. An absent day takes off one day's pay, a half-day half of it. **Leave beyond the month's paid-leave quota counts as absent.** Alternatives some shops use: a fixed 30-day base (every month the same day's pay), or a 26-day base (working days, Sundays left out); either could be a setting later.
- **Daily wage:** paid for each present day; a half-day pays half. Weekly offs and holidays are unpaid.
- **Late:** recorded and shown on the slip, **no automatic pay cut**. The owner can add a deduction with a reason.
- **Overtime:** off by default; the owner switches it on per person with a rate in ₹ per hour.
- **Rounding:** worked out exactly in paise (ADR 0008); the net pay is rounded to the nearest rupee with a round-off line, like bills (`02` #6).
- **Labour law:** the app records the owner's choices and **doesn't check labour law** (state overtime rates, what may be deducted). *Unverified, check the current Labour Codes:* the Code on Wages probably caps a month's total deductions at 50% of the wages, and allows fines only for acts notified in advance and up to 3% of the wages, so a fine for being late may not be allowed. The app can show a warning; it doesn't block.

**Why:** dividing by the month's real days is common and easy to check by hand; no automatic cuts means no surprises for staff.

**Effect:** these are P2.4's business rules (Day 6), each with tests: months of 28, 29, 30 and 31 days, half-days, leave beyond the quota, joining or leaving mid-month, overtime on and off, round-off.

### 05 Q3: what if an advance is bigger than one month's salary?

**Recommended:** allowed. Each person has one running advance balance (every advance adds to it; recoveries reduce it), no interest. The owner sets a recovery amount per month, or "all at once". **A month's recovery is capped at 50% of that month's pay by default** (the owner can change the cap; see "Labour law" in Q2), and never takes the pay below zero: whatever doesn't fit carries to the next month by itself. The owner can change the instalment at any time. Giving advances is a feature switch that starts **off** for staff (`00` #17).

**Why:** that's how shops really work, it guarantees a slip never shows a negative salary, and the 50% default leaves the person enough to live on (and probably matches the Code on Wages, unverified). It is the owner's brief exactly: enter the advance, and the app works out what is still to be returned.

**Effect:** P2.4 rules and tests (carry-forward, the 50% cap, several advances, a change of instalment). When someone leaves with an advance left, the balance stays visible until it's settled (paid back, or written off by the owner with a reason).

### 05 Q4: can a staff member work in more than one branch?

**Recommended:** close it as answered by `00` #35: one home branch for attendance and salary; the owner can let a login work in other branches. A day worked at another branch is marked present in the home branch's register.

**Effect:** none.

### 05 Q5: statutory deductions (PF, ESI): in scope or not?

**Recommended:** **out of v1**; noted in `ideas-backlog.md`.

**Why:** PF and ESI generally apply from 20 and 10 employees, which most shops this app is for don't reach, and ESI covers only staff earning up to ₹21,000 a month. Their ceilings and rates change and would each need exact tests. *Figures to check against the current Labour Codes.* The one-off deduction line (draft ideas) covers a shop that wants to show one.

**Effect:** keeps P2.4 to one session.

### Claude's additions (proposed)

- **S1. Salary month:** the calendar month (1st to last day), paid after it ends. Joining or leaving mid-month pays only the days between.
- **S2. A finalized month is locked.** Once a month's salary is finalized, its attendance can't change; a correction becomes an addition or deduction on the next month, with a reason. Same idea as "a saved bill is never edited" (`02` #14).
- **S3. Salary changes** (a raise) start from the 1st of a month; old rates are kept, never overwritten, so old slips stay right.
- **S4. Working on a weekly off or holiday** pays one extra day's pay (monthly staff) or the normal day's wage (daily staff).
- **S5. Who may do what:** setting salaries, giving advances, paying salaries and marking attendance are feature switches that start **off** for staff; the owner can switch each on per staff login (`00` #17). *If you want any of them owner-only for good, say so: that is an exception to `00` #17 and needs a line in `00`'s Change log.*
- **S6. Staff record fields:** name, phone, home branch, joining date, salary type and amount, weekly off day, paid leaves per month, overtime rate if any.
- **S7. Entry log:** marking or changing attendance (a change to a past day needs a reason), salary set or changed, advance given, recovery changed, salary paid, month finalized.
- **S8. Leaving:** deactivating a person (`00` #34) offers a final settlement slip: pay up to the last day, minus any advance left.
- **S9. The AI's "holidays" question** (`09`) counts absent days plus leave days; weekly offs and shop holidays are not "taken".
