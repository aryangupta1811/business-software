- **Date:** 2026-11-20 (fast track Day 4, lane 3)
- **Status:** Approved by the owner on 2026-11-20, with all seven questions in section 12 answered as recommended (see "Owner's answers").
- **Requirements:** Module 01 (DECIDED): #1 (low-stock level per branch), #2 and #16 (units), #4 (average cost), #9 (opening stock, manual part), #10 (stock goes up only through purchases after opening stock), #11 (adjustments with a reason, logged, by permission), #12 (negative stock: warn, owner can switch to block). - **ADRs it builds on:** 0007 (rules 3, 4, 6, 7: one transaction, stock as a ledger with a locked current-stock row, lock order by item ID, idempotency keys), 0008 (paise, thousandths), 0027 (tenancy), 0028 (entry log), 0036 (Zod bodies, declared permissions), 0037 (`backup_read` on every tenant table).
- **Plan:** `docs/plans/2026-11-20-p1.8-stock-ledger.md` (written after the OK)

## 1. Goal and success criteria

Every item of kind `goods` has a stock quantity per branch that changes only through a recorded movement. P1.8 builds the ledger, opening stock and adjustments. Sales (P1.14), returns (P1.16), purchases (P2.1), transfers and counts (P1.11), batches, variants and serial numbers (P1.10) all write through the same path later, without changing these tables.

Done when:

1. A branch's stock for an item equals the sum of its movements, always. A test checks this after every stock test, and the same query is a one-line audit for the evening check.
2. Opening stock and adjustments each write one movement, update the current-stock row and write one entry log row, in one transaction.
3. A `stock.adjusted` entry stores the quantity, the value at cost, the reason and the business date (`06` #7).
4. Movements can't be edited or deleted by the app (the database refuses, `42501`).
5. Two counters changing the same item at the same branch at the same time both land, in some order, and the final stock is exact. Two counters changing two items in opposite order never deadlock.
6. A request that waits too long for another counter's lock gets a clean `503 busy` and gives its connection back. A transaction left open is ended by the database.
7. A retried request (same idempotency key) returns the first result and moves stock once.
8. Average cost follows each movement, so purchases (P2.1) and sales (P1.14) only have to call the same helper.
9. The Items page shows stock, the low-stock state, opening stock, an adjustment form and the history, in three languages, light and dark, at 1024 px.

## 3. Quantities and cost

- **Quantities** are thousandths of the item's stock unit (ADR 0008, P1.7 design section 3), signed in the ledger: `+5 kg` is `5000`, `-250 g` from a kg item is `-250`.
- **Whole units** (question 6): for stock units `piece`, `pair`, `dozen`, `box`, `pack` and `bag` a quantity must be a whole number of units (a multiple of 1000). `kg`, `g`, `litre`, `ml`, `metre` and `foot` allow three decimals. One shared function, `checkStockQuantity(unit, quantity)`, used by the API and by every later task.
- **Average cost** (Module 01 #4), per item per branch, in paise per stock unit, kept on the current-stock row:
  - **Stock in** (opening stock, an upward count correction, later a purchase): `new average = (old quantity × old average + quantity × unit cost) / new quantity`, rounded half-up to the paisa. If the old quantity was 0 or below, the new average is simply the incoming unit cost (the old average describes stock that isn't there).
  - **Stock out** (adjustment down, later a sale): the movement's unit cost is the current average; the average doesn't change.
  - **Value** of a movement = `quantity × unit cost / 1000`, rounded half-up to the paisa, signed.
  - **Per branch, not per business:** each branch paid its own prices, and a transfer (P1.11) moves stock at the sending branch's average. If you'd rather have one average for the whole business, say so with the answers.
  - **Medical** uses the batch's own cost (`01` #15), added with batches in P1.10. Until then medical items use the average like everyone else.
- **No known cost:** opening stock takes a unit cost, defaulting to the item's `purchase_price`. If the item has none and the caller can't see costs, the request is refused (`cost_required`), so stock never arrives valued at zero by accident.

## 4. Data (migration number fixed at rebase; lane 2's login migration takes `0008`)

Both tables follow ADR 0027: `id()`, `business_id` defaulting to the transaction's business, `tenantPolicies` (with `backup_read`, ADR 0037), hand-written `FORCE`, composite keys into tenant tables, `unique (business_id, id)`. App code never runs `DELETE`.

### `item_stock` (current stock, one row per item per branch)

| Column | Type | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `id`, `business_id`, `created_at` | | ADR 0027 |
| `branch_id`, `item_id` | uuid | composite keys to `branches` and `items`; unique `(business_id, branch_id, item_id)` |
| `quantity` | bigint | thousandths, may be negative (`01` #12 allows selling past 0) |
| `average_cost` | bigint, null | paise per stock unit, ≥ 0; null until the first stock in |
| `last_movement_id` | uuid | the movement that last changed the row (a cheap check in tests) |
| `updated_at` | timestamptz | |

- Created with `insert … on conflict do nothing`, then locked with `select … for update`. Two counters creating the same row at once: the second waits on the unique index, then finds the row.
- A service item never gets a row: `moveStock` reads the item's kind in the same transaction and refuses a service.

### `stock_movements` (the ledger, append-only)

| Column | Type | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `id`, `business_id`, `created_at` | | ADR 0027; UUIDv7, so a branch's history pages by `id` |
| `branch_id`, `item_id` | uuid | composite keys |
| `kind` | text | one of `STOCK_MOVEMENT_KINDS` in `packages/shared`: `opening`, `adjustment` today; `sale`, `sale_return`, `purchase`, `purchase_return`, `transfer_out`, `transfer_in`, `count` are added by their tasks (the check is regenerated from the list) |
| `quantity` | bigint | thousandths, signed, `<> 0` |
| `unit_cost` | bigint, null | paise per stock unit at that moment (section 3); null only for an item with no known cost at a stock-out |
| `value` | bigint, null | paise, signed, `quantity × unit_cost / 1000` |
| `balance_after` | bigint | the branch's stock after this movement; the history shows it, and a test checks the chain |
| `business_date` | date | the day in IST the action belongs to (`06` #7). From an injectable business clock, so P1.20's seed can backdate it; `created_at` stays the database's time |
| `reason_code`, `note` | text, null | adjustments only (section 5); a check requires `reason_code` when `kind = 'adjustment'` |
| `actor_id` | uuid | composite key to `users`; who did it |
| `request_key` | uuid, null | idempotency (ADR 0007 rule 7); unique `(business_id, request_key) where request_key is not null` |

- **Append-only:** the migration revokes `UPDATE` from `app_runtime`, and `stock_movements` joins the guard's `APPEND_ONLY` list. A mistake is fixed by a new movement.
- **Where a movement came from:** later tasks add a nullable composite key per document (`bill_id`, `purchase_id`, `transfer_id`, `count_id`) and a check that the kind matches. These are additive migrations; the P1.8 columns don't change.
- **Index:** `(business_id, branch_id, item_id, id)` for the history and the audit sum.

### Setting on `businesses`

`negative_stock text not null default 'warn'`, check `in ('warn', 'block')` (`01` #12; scope is question 1). Changed in Settings by the owner, logged as `business.updated` like the other settings.

### Why a ledger *and* a current-stock row

ADR 0007 rule 4 already decides this; the reason, for the interview: the ledger is the truth and the history (who, when, why, at what cost), and nothing in it is ever overwritten. The current-stock row is a running total so billing doesn't add up years of movements on every scan, and it is the one row two counters lock, so they queue per item per branch instead of blocking each other.

### Why app code, not a database trigger, keeps the two in step

One helper in `packages/db`, `moveStock(tx, movements)`, is the only code that writes either table, like `recordEntry` is for the log (`stock_movements` and `item_stock` aren't exported for writing). A trigger would also keep them in step, but it would hide the locking from the code that must take locks in item-ID order across a whole bill, and it can't refuse a bad sale with a message the counter understands. The "stock = sum of movements" test after every stock test is the safety net.

## 5. Adjustments and opening stock

**Adjustment** (`01` #11): an item, a quantity change (+ or −), a reason from a fixed list and an optional note.

Recommended reasons (question 3), each with its allowed direction:

| Reason | Direction |
| --- | --- |
| Damaged | down |
| Expired | down |
| Theft or lost | down |
| Own use (taken for the shop or home) | down |
| Count mismatch | up or down |
| Other (note required) | down |

- **An adjustment never takes stock below zero** (question 2): you can't write off stock the app doesn't think you have. If stock is already negative (sold before the purchase was entered), only "Count mismatch" upward is allowed.
- **Value at cost** is stored in the movement and copied into the `stock.adjusted` entry (`06` #7, question 5).
- Archived items and services are refused (`409 archived`, `409 service_has_no_stock`).

**Opening stock** (`01` #9, #10): a quantity and a unit cost for one item at one branch.

- Allowed only while the item has **no movements at that branch** (question 4). After that, a mistake is fixed with a "Count mismatch" adjustment, so the history shows it.
- Writes one `opening` movement and logs `stock.opening_set` (new, reason optional).
- P1.9's Excel import uses the same rule and the same helper.

**Archiving an item with stock** (question 7): allowed; the confirm dialog shows the stock left at each branch. The stock and its history stay; restoring the item brings them back.

### Entry log

| Action | Reason | `after` holds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `stock.adjusted` (exists, reason required) | the reason's label plus the note | `quantity`, `balanceAfter`, `unitCost`, `value`, `reasonCode`, `businessDate`, `movementId` |
| `stock.opening_set` (new, reason optional) | | `quantity`, `unitCost`, `value`, `businessDate`, `movementId` |

The log's `branch_id` is the movement's branch. **P2.6 must hide `unitCost` and `value` from viewers without `items.view_cost`**, as it hides the purchase price (added to P2.6's notes).

## 6. The write path: `moveStock`

```
moveStock(tx, { branchId, actorId, businessDate, requestKey?, lines: [{ itemId, kind, quantity, unitCost?, reasonCode?, note? }] })
  → { movements, crossings }
```

1. Sort lines by item ID (ADR 0007 rule 6), so any two calls lock in the same order.
2. For each item: create the `item_stock` row if missing, lock it `for update`, read the item's kind and archive state.
3. Apply the rules (section 5; later tasks add theirs, e.g. P1.14's warn/block), compute unit cost, value, the new average and `balance_after`.
4. Insert the movements in one statement and update each `item_stock` row.
5. Return **crossings**: items whose stock went from above the branch's low-stock level to at or below it, or from 0 or above to below 0, and back. P1.8 only returns them; the low-stock and out-of-stock alerts (`07` #4) are written here in one place once P2.7 part 2's alerts table exists.

The service around it opens the transaction (`withBusiness`), checks the idempotency key first (a stored movement with that key: answer the original result; the same key with a different body: `422 key_reused`), calls `moveStock`, then `recordEntry`.

## 7. Locks and timeouts

**Row locks on `item_stock`, in item-ID order** (ADR 0007 rules 4 and 6). Considered and not chosen:

- `SERIALIZABLE` transactions: correct, but every conflicting bill fails and must be retried as a whole; with two busy counters that's wasted work at the moment the customer is waiting.
- Advisory locks: not tied to a row, so a forgotten lock is silent, and they'd need their own ordering rule.

**Timeouts for `app_runtime`** (from the 2026-11-10 scan), set in `createDatabase` as connection start-up options for the API and worker pools only (migrations keep no limit):

| Setting | Starting value | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `lock_timeout` | 3 s | a bill saves in under 1 s (NFR 5), so a 3 s wait means something is stuck; better a clear "busy, try again" than a frozen counter |
| `statement_timeout` | 10 s | no counter query should come close; stops a runaway query holding a connection |
| `idle_in_transaction_session_timeout` | 15 s | a transaction left open by a bug is ended and its locks released |

- Start-up options, not `ALTER ROLE`, because role settings need the RDS master and a hand-run step on every environment; options travel with the code and a test reads them back (`show lock_timeout`) through the real pool.
- The values are confirmed by the two-counter tests (section 10) and recorded in a short ADR on database timeouts (number at merge).
- Errors: `55P03` (lock timeout) answers `503 busy` with `Retry-After: 1`; the screen retries once with the same idempotency key. `57014` (statement timeout) stays a `500` with one log line.

## 8. Permissions and API

New in `PERMISSIONS` (the owner has all; P1.5 decides staff defaults):

| Permission | Allows |
| --- | --- |
| `stock.view` | see quantities and the history |
| `stock.adjust` | opening stock and adjustments (`01` #11: only staff with permission) |

Cost and value fields are left out entirely without `items.view_cost` (P1.7's rule), and a body carrying `unitCost` without it answers `403`.

| Route | Permission | Does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `GET /api/items/:id/stock` | `stock.view` | per branch: quantity, low-stock level, `low`, `canOpen` (no movements yet); `averageCost` and value with `items.view_cost` |
| `GET /api/items/:id/stock/movements?branch=&after=` | `stock.view` | history, newest first, 50 at a time, keyset on `id` |
| `POST /api/items/:id/stock/opening` | `stock.adjust` | `{ quantity, unitCost?, requestKey }` at the caller's branch |
| `POST /api/items/:id/stock/adjustments` | `stock.adjust` | `{ quantity, reasonCode, note?, requestKey }` at the caller's branch |
| `GET /api/items` (existing) | `items.view` | adds `stock` and `low` at the caller's branch when the caller has `stock.view`; new filter `low=true` |
| Settings (existing) | `settings.manage` | adds `negativeStock` |

Error codes: `archived`, `service_has_no_stock`, `opening_exists`, `below_zero`, `whole_units_only`, `wrong_direction`, `note_required`, `cost_required`, `key_reused`, `busy`.

The branch is the caller's (`Caller.branchId`); P1.6 adds switching branches.

## 9. Room left for later tasks

- **P1.14 sales, P1.16 returns:** `moveStock` lines with `kind: 'sale'` at the average cost; the warn/block rule reads `businesses.negative_stock`; a `bill_id` key on movements. Locks already come in item-ID order for a whole bill.
- **P2.1 purchases:** `kind: 'purchase'` with the entry's unit cost; the average formula is already there.
- **P1.11 transfers and counts:** two movements (out at the sender's average, in at the same cost) under one `transfer_id`; a count writes `count` movements for the differences.
- **P1.10 batches and variants:** a sub-balance table per batch (`stock_batches`: branch, item, batch number, expiry, MRP, cost, quantity) and per variant, with a nullable `batch_id`/`variant_id` on movements. `item_stock` stays the item's total, still the row that's locked first; batch rows are locked after it, by ID. A test then checks batches sum to the total.
- **P1.10 serial numbers:** a `serial_numbers` table (one row per unit, status, the movement that brought it in and the one that took it out); each such movement is ±1000.

None of these change a P1.8 column.

## 10. Tests (test-first for every rule)

**`packages/shared` (unit):**
- `checkStockQuantity`: whole units for piece, pair, dozen, box, pack, bag; three decimals for kg, g, litre, ml, metre, foot; zero refused.
- Average cost: two stock-ins at different costs; rounding half-up at .5 paise; old quantity 0 or negative resets to the incoming cost; a stock-out leaves the average unchanged; value rounding for a 250 g line at a kg price.
- The adjustment schema: reason list, direction per reason, note required for "Other", unknown fields refused, `unitCost` refused in the staff variant.

**`packages/db` (Testcontainers):**
- `stock_movements`: `UPDATE` and `DELETE` refused as `app_runtime` (`42501`); `quantity = 0` refused; an adjustment without `reason_code` refused; a duplicate `request_key` refused.
- Cross-business: business B can't point a movement or a stock row at A's item, branch or user.
- Guard: `stock_movements` on `APPEND_ONLY`; both tables pass every rule, `backup_read` included.
- `moveStock`: balance and `balance_after` chain; stock = sum of movements after each test; crossings returned for low, out, back above.
- **Two counters:** 20 concurrent adjustments of +1 on one item at one branch end at exactly +20 with 20 rows and an unbroken `balance_after` chain; two transactions moving items X then Y and Y then X both finish (no `40P01`).
- **Timeouts:** a raw transaction holds the stock row past `lock_timeout` → the second gets `55P03` and its connection returns to the pool; a transaction left idle is ended after `idle_in_transaction_session_timeout`; `show` reads all three values through `createDatabase`.

**`apps/api`:**
- Opening stock: once per item per branch (`409 opening_exists` after any movement), cost defaulting to the purchase price, `cost_required`, one `stock.opening_set` entry.
- Adjustments: each reason's direction, `below_zero`, `whole_units_only`, archived and service refused, one `stock.adjusted` entry with value and business date.
- Idempotency: the same key twice moves stock once and answers the same body; the same key with another body is `422 key_reused`.
- Permissions: `stock.view` and `stock.adjust` enforced; cost and value absent without `items.view_cost` (key absent, not null); `403` for a body with `unitCost`.
- `503 busy` with `Retry-After` on a lock timeout.
- Items list `stock`, `low` and the `low=true` filter; settings `negativeStock` saved and logged.
- The route census picks up the new routes (ADR 0036).

**`apps/web`:**
- The adjustment form: reasons in three languages, direction shown by reason, note required for "Other", whole-unit errors under the field, retry with the same key on `503`.
- Opening stock shown only while `canOpen`.

**Real-app check in Chrome:** three languages, light and dark, 1024 px, no CSP violations; the database rows, the entry log and the stock audit query after a few adjustments.

## 12. Questions for the owner

Each has a recommended answer. None changes a DECIDED point; they fill in what `01` #9–12 and `06` #7 leave open.

1. **Negative stock: one switch for the whole business, or per item?** `01` #12 decided: billing is allowed with a warning, and the owner can switch it to block. It doesn't say for what.
   - A. One switch for the whole business, in Settings. **Recommended:** simple, and it's what #12 describes.
   - B. Per item.
   - C. A business switch plus a per-item override (more screens, can be added later without changing data).
2. **Can an adjustment take stock below zero?** (Billing's warn/block is #12; this is about write-offs.)
   - A. Never. **Recommended:** you can't write off stock the app doesn't think you have; it keeps the "big write-down" flag (`06` #7) honest.
   - B. Follow the same warn/block switch as billing.
3. **The list of adjustment reasons.** `01` #11 names damage, theft, expiry and counting mismatch.
   - A. Exactly those four; only "Count mismatch" can add stock.
   - B. Those four plus "Own use" (down) and "Other" (down, note required); only "Count mismatch" can add stock. **Recommended:** covers taking goods for the shop or home, and "Other" with a note catches the rest without letting stock grow outside purchases (`01` #10).
   - C. B, plus the owner can add their own reasons.
4. **When can opening stock be typed?**
   - A. Only while the item has no stock movements at that branch; after that, a "Count mismatch" adjustment fixes mistakes. **Recommended:** opening stock can't become a back door around #10, and every correction is in the history.
   - B. Editable until the item's first sale.
5. **Value of a write-down.** `06` #7 says "value at purchase price"; `01` #4 says profit uses the average cost.
   - A. The average cost of the stock at that branch, falling back to the item's purchase price when there's no stock history. **Recommended:** it is what that stock actually cost, and it matches the profit numbers.
   - B. The item's current purchase price field.
6. **Decimals in stock.**
   - A. Whole numbers for piece, pair, dozen, box, pack and bag; up to 3 decimals for kg, g, litre, ml, metre and foot. **Recommended:** half a piece is always a typing mistake; 0.250 kg is normal.
   - B. Decimals allowed for every unit.
7. **Archiving an item that still has stock.**
   - A. Allowed; the confirm shows the stock left at each branch, and stock and history stay with the item. **Recommended:** archiving hides an item, it isn't a write-off.
   - B. Refused until stock is zero (forces a write-down that may not be real).
