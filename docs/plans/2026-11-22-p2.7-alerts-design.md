## 1. Goal and success criteria

The owner and the staff see, without anyone checking, which items are running low or have run out at their branch, in a bell that never interrupts billing. The same machinery carries every later alert type.

Done when:

1. Selling the last items above an item's low-stock level raises **one** low-stock alert for that item at that branch; selling more does not raise another. Selling the last one turns it into out of stock. Restocking above the level clears it by itself (`07` #4).
2. Changing an item's low-stock level raises or clears its alert at once, without waiting for the next sale.
3. The top bar shows a bell with a count. Opening it shows the alerts grouped by type ("7 items low on stock"), each with its item, stock, level and, with 2+ branches, its branch. Nothing pops up while billing (`07` #6).
4. Each person can dismiss an alert for themselves; others still see it (`07` #6).
5. The owner sees every branch's alerts; staff see only the branch they're logged in at, and only the types they may see (`07` #2, #7).
6. The owner can switch each type off for the app in Settings; the default comes from the business type (`07` #3, `00` #1).
7. Nothing is deleted: a cleared alert stays as history for the daily summary (P2.8) and the dashboard.
8. One business never sees another's alerts (a test over two businesses).

## 3. How it works today

- Every stock change goes through `moveStock` (`packages/db/src/stock.ts`), which already knows each line's `balanceBefore` and `balanceAfter` inside the transaction.
- Levels live in `item_branch_levels` (one per item per branch). `isLow(quantity, level)` in shared says "low" when a level above 0 is set and the stock is at or under it.
- The business's features include `expiry_alerts` (the medical preset). No alert table, route or screen exists yet.

## 4. Approaches: when an alert is raised

- **A. In the same transaction as the change.** **Recommended.** `moveStock` and the level update compare before and after and raise, change or clear the alert row right there. The alert can never disagree with the stock: both commit together or neither does. It costs one small upsert per line that crosses a level, and nothing for lines that don't. No worker is involved, so alerts work even while the worker is stopped.
- **B. Through the outbox and the worker.** The stock change writes an event; the worker raises the alert later. It keeps writes slightly lighter, but alerts arrive late, the worker must be running, and it needs the cross-business read that isn't designed yet.
- **C. Worked out when someone looks.** Compare stock with levels on every bell request. There is no row to remember "raised once" or a dismissal against, so `07` #4 and #6 can't be met without inventing one anyway.

Time-based alerts (expiry, overdue udhaar) have no change to hang on; they will be raised by a daily worker job that calls the same raise and clear functions (P1.10, P1.18).

## 5. Data

### `alerts` (new table, branch-level, RLS like every table)

| Column | Type | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `id`, `business_id`, `branch_id` | | Composite key to `branches` (ADR 0027). |
| `type` | text | `low_stock`, `out_of_stock` now; later types add their name (checked by Zod, not the database, like permissions). |
| `subject_type`, `subject_id` | text, uuid | What it is about: `item` and the item's id now; `batch`, `customer`, `entry` later. |
| `details` | jsonb | What the bell shows, fixed when raised or changed: stock and level for stock alerts. |
| `raised_at`, `updated_at` | timestamptz | `updated_at` moves when the details or type change (low → out). |
| `cleared_at` | timestamptz, null | Set when the condition ends. A cleared alert is history; never deleted. |
| unique | | One **open** alert per subject per branch per family: a partial unique index on `(business_id, branch_id, subject_type, subject_id, family) where cleared_at is null`, where `family` is `stock` for both stock types. So low and out are one alert that changes type, never two at once (question 3). |

### `alert_dismissals`

`business_id`, `alert_id`, `user_id`, `dismissed_at`; composite keys; unique per alert and person. A dismissal belongs to one alert row, so when an alert clears and a new one is raised later, the new one shows again (question 2).

### The rule for stock (shared, pure, tested on its own)

`stockAlert(quantity, level)` answers `out_of_stock` when the quantity is 0 or below (an item that is tracked and has a level or is sold out), `low_stock` when `isLow`, else none. Then `raiseOrClear` turns "what it should be" plus "what is open" into insert, change type, update details or clear. An item with no level set gets out-of-stock alerts only (question 4). Archiving an item clears its open alerts.

### Switches

- **Per business, per type, for the app** (`07` #3): a new `alert_settings` jsonb on `businesses`, or a column per type; defaults from the business-type preset. A type switched off **still records** alerts (the summary and the dashboard need them); the switch only hides them in the app. Turning it back on shows what is open right now. My call, so turning a switch off never loses history.
- **Who sees which type:** stock alerts need `stock.view` (a cashier who may not see stock doesn't learn it from the bell; question 1). Money and flag alerts get their own staff switches, off by default, when those types land (`07` #2).

## 6. API

| Route | Needs | Does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `GET /api/alerts` | `session.own`, filtered by type permission | Open alerts the caller may see and hasn't dismissed: owner all branches, staff their session's branch. Grouped counts plus the rows (item name, branch, stock, level). Light enough to poll. |
| `POST /api/alerts/:id/dismiss` | `session.own` | Dismisses it for the caller only. Not in the entry log (it changes nothing in the business). |
| `GET`/`PUT /api/settings/alerts` | `settings.view` / `settings.manage` | The per-type app switches. Changing them is logged (`settings.alerts_changed`). |

Raising and clearing are not routes; they happen inside `moveStock` and the level update. They are not entry-log rows either: the stock change that caused them already is one.

## 7. Screens

- **The bell** in the top bar, with a count of what the person hasn't dismissed. It refreshes every 60 seconds and when the window gets focus (the same rhythm as the billing catalogue). No sound, no pop-up, no toast (`07` #6). On the billing page it stays a plain count.
- **The panel** opens from the bell (a native `<dialog>`, keyboard-reachable through the shortcuts registry): one group per type ("Out of stock (2)", "Low on stock (7)"), each row with item, stock, level, branch (with 2+ branches) and a dismiss button. An item opens its Stock page.
- **Settings → Alerts:** a row per type with an App switch, and a line saying WhatsApp comes with the daily summary.
- All text in English, Hindi and Hinglish.

## 8. Tests (test-first)

- **Shared:**
  - `stockAlert`: no level, level 0, at the level, under it, 0, below 0;
  - `raiseOrClear`: every transition (none → low, low → low with new stock, low → out, out → low, low → none, out → none).
- **Database:**
  - the partial unique index refuses a second open stock alert for the same item and branch;
  - composite keys refuse another business's branch;
  - the tenancy guard covers both new tables.
- **Through `moveStock`:**
  - a sale crossing the level raises one alert;
  - a second sale under it raises none;
  - selling to 0 changes it to out of stock;
  - a purchase or adjustment back above clears it;
  - two lines of one bill for two items raise two;
  - the alert and the stock roll back together when the bill fails.
- **Level change:**
  - raising the level above the stock raises the alert;
  - lowering it under the stock clears it.
- **API:**
  - staff see only their branch;
  - staff without `stock.view` see no stock alerts;
  - the owner sees all branches;
  - a dismissal hides it for one person only;
  - a new alert after a clear shows again;
  - switching a type off hides it while it is still recorded, and switching it on shows it again;
  - archiving an item clears its alert;
  - two businesses never see each other's alerts.
- **Web:**
  - the bell count;
  - groups and dismiss;
  - no dialog opens by itself on the billing page;
  - the refresh on focus;
  - the Settings switches;
  - three languages.

## 9. Questions for the owner

**Answered 2026-11-22:** the owner took all five recommendations (1A to 5A). They are `07` #14–#18.

Each has a recommended answer. None changes a DECIDED point.

1. **Staff who may not see stock** (`stock.view` off).
   - A. They get no stock alerts. **Recommended:** a bell that says "Sugar: 2 left" shows the stock the owner chose to hide.
   - B. They still get them, as `07` #2 lists stock alerts for staff without exceptions.

2. **Dismissing an alert.**
   - A. It stays hidden for you until it clears; if the item runs low again later, it shows again. **Recommended:** a dismissal answers "I know about this one", not "never tell me about this item".
   - B. It comes back after 24 hours if still open.

3. **Low and out of stock for the same item.**
   - A. One alert that changes from "low" to "out" and back. **Recommended:** the bell shows each item once, in its worst state.
   - B. Two separate alerts.

4. **Items with no low-stock level set.**
   - A. They still get an out-of-stock alert when they reach 0. **Recommended:** running out matters even when nobody set a level, and most new shops won't have set levels yet.
   - B. No alerts at all until a level is set.

5. **The bell on the billing page.**
   - A. It shows the count like everywhere else, and opens only if someone clicks it. **Recommended:** a cashier mid-bill is never interrupted (`07` #6), and can still see it after the customer leaves.
   - B. Hidden on the billing page.
