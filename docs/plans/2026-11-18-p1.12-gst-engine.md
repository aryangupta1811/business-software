# P1.12 GST engine: plan

**Goal:** one pure function, `calculateBill`, that turns a cart into every number a GST bill needs: each line's discount, taxable value and tax; the bill totals; the round-off; and summaries by rate and by HSN code. It lives in `packages/shared`, so the billing screen (P1.13), the server's re-check when a bill is saved (P1.14), the printed bill (P1.15) and the GST export (P1.17) all use the same code and can never disagree.

**Not in this task:** no UI, no database, no item lookup, no MRP or staff-limit checks (P1.13, which can use this engine's per-line totals), no invoice numbers (P1.14).

**Sources:** Module 02 #1–6 (DECIDED 2026-11-17), Module 00 #6 (bill type by GST status), Module 01 #2–3 (loose quantities, MRP), ADR 0008 (integer paise, rates in basis points, quantities in thousandths, one allocation function, every rounding rule named).

## API

```ts
calculateBill(input: BillInput): Bill                              // gst.ts
allocatePaise(total: Paise, weights: readonly Paise[]): Paise[]   // money.ts
INDIAN_STATES, isStateCode(code), stateByCode(code)               // states.ts
```

- `INDIAN_STATES` (added at the coordinator's request, so P1.3 signup can use the same list): the 28 states, 8 Union Territories and Other Territory, each `{ code, name, utgst }`, with the two-digit GST state codes (the first two digits of a GSTIN). Left out: 25 (Daman and Diu, merged into 26 in 2020), 28 (Andhra Pradesh before 2014, now 37), 96 (Foreign Country; exports aren't in v1) and 99 (Centre Jurisdiction). Names are English; the web app translates them by code.

- `BillInput`: `scheme` (`'regular' | 'composition' | 'unregistered'`), `supplierStateCode` and `placeOfSupplyStateCode` (codes from `INDIAN_STATES`, e.g. `"09"` for Uttar Pradesh), `lines`, optional `billDiscount`.
- Each line: `unitPricePaise`, `quantityThousandths` (0.250 kg → `250`), `rateBasisPoints` (18% → `1800`), `priceIncludesTax` (a real boolean), `hsn` (4, 6 or 8 digits; may be empty only for a composition or unregistered business, whose bill needs none), optional `discount`.
- A discount is `{ kind: 'percent', basisPoints }` (10.5% → `1050`) or `{ kind: 'amount', paise }`. An item discount is off the whole line (not per unit), in the line's own price basis. A bill discount in rupees is what the customer pays less (rule 3).
- `Bill`: `tax` (`'cgst-sgst' | 'igst' | 'none'`), `stateTaxName` (`'SGST' | 'UTGST'`, or `null` unless CGST + SGST is charged), per-line results, totals, `roundOffPaise`, `grandTotalPaise`, `byRate` and `byHsn` summaries.
- **Every money field has one meaning,** so the printed bill adds up. A line's `amountPaise` and `itemDiscountPaise` are in its own price basis, as printed on the line. Everything else is what the customer pays, tax included: `grossPaise` (no discount at all), `subtotalPaise` (after item discounts), `billDiscountPaise` (how much less the customer pays because of the bill discount) and `totalPaise`, except `taxablePaise` and the taxes. The bill's fields are sums of its lines', and gross − (gross − subtotal) − bill discount = total = taxable + taxes; total + round-off = grand total.
- Bad input (negative amounts, a quantity of 0, a discount bigger than what it's taken from, a state code not in `INDIAN_STATES`, a rate outside 0–100%, a malformed HSN code, a `priceIncludesTax` that isn't a boolean, a composition or unregistered business selling to another state, a result too big for a safe integer) throws a `RangeError`. The server's re-check (P1.14) gets untyped JSON, so these are checked at run time. The screen validates first; the engine refuses rather than guesses.

## Rules, in the order they apply

| # | Rule | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Line amount** = unit price × quantity ÷ 1000, rounded to the paisa, half up. | ADR 0008 (quantities in thousandths) |
| 2 | **Item discount** is taken from the line amount. A percent discount is rounded half up. | Module 02 #5 |
| 3 | **Bill discount**, shared across the lines by largest remainder (the leftover paise go to the lines with the biggest remainders, earlier line first on a tie), so the shares add up exactly. **In rupees (₹X): the customer pays exactly ₹X less** (owner, 2026-11-18). ₹X is shared by what the customer pays for each line (tax included); each line that gets a share is then priced at its old payable minus its share, as a tax-inclusive price (rule 8), so its total drops by exactly its share and the bill's by exactly ₹X before round-off. For a tax-exclusive line that's the same as taking about share × 100% ÷ (100% + rate) off its taxable value. **As a percent:** worked out once on the subtotal of the lines' own-basis amounts (half up) and shared by those amounts, so each line loses that percent of its own price. | Module 02 #5 (owner, 2026-11-18); ADR 0008 (allocation function) |
| 4 | **GST on the price after both discounts.** Discounts shown on the invoice at the time of sale are not part of the taxable value. | Module 02 #5; CGST Act s. 15(3)(a) |
| 5 | **Place of supply:** same state code → CGST + SGST; different → IGST. The caller passes the place of supply (for a counter sale, the branch's state; for a delivery, where it ends). | Module 02 #3; IGST Act ss. 7–8, 10(1) |
| 6 | **UTGST:** for the five Union Territories without a legislature (Chandigarh 04, Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu 26, Lakshadweep 31, Andaman and Nicobar 35, Ladakh 38) and Other Territory (97), the state half is called UTGST (`utgst: true` in `INDIAN_STATES`). Delhi, Puducherry and Jammu and Kashmir keep SGST. | UTGST Act s. 7 |
| 7 | **Tax-exclusive price:** taxable = net amount; each tax = taxable × its rate, half up. CGST and SGST are each worked out on their own (half the rate each), so they are always equal. | Module 02 #4; CGST Act s. 9 and SGST/UTGST levy at equal rates |
| 8 | **Tax-inclusive price:** the customer pays exactly the net amount (a shelf or MRP price is never exceeded by a paisa). Each tax = net × its rate ÷ (100% + full rate), half up; taxable = net − taxes. CGST and SGST stay equal; any odd paisa stays in the taxable value. **The trade-off:** with the price exact and the halves equal, a printed tax can differ by at most 1 paisa per tax from rate × the printed taxable value, rounded (₹52.12 at 0.25%: taxable ₹52.00, each half 6 paise, while 0.125% of ₹52.00 is 6.5 → 7). The taxable value is within 1 paisa of the exact price ÷ (100% + rate). Both bounds are tested. | Module 02 #4, Module 01 #3 |
| 9 | **Rounding per line:** each line's tax is rounded to the paisa, and every total is the sum of the line figures, so the lines, the totals and the summaries always agree. | ADR 0008 (rounding inside shared, named); common invoice practice, Rule 46(i)–(j) shows tax per item |
| 10 | **Composition and unregistered businesses charge no tax:** the price is the price (inclusive or exclusive makes no difference), every tax is 0, and summaries group under rate 0. The bill is a bill of supply or a plain bill. **They can't sell to another state:** the engine refuses it. | Module 00 #6; CGST Act ss. 10(4), 10(2)(c) (no inter-state supply under composition), 24(i) (an inter-state seller of goods must register) |
| 11 | **Round-off** to the nearest rupee, 50 paise and up rounds up, on its own line: grand total = total + round-off, with round-off between −49 and +50 paise. Applies to every scheme. | Module 02 #6; CGST Act s. 170 (nearest rupee) |
| 12 | **Summaries:** `byRate` groups lines by rate; `byHsn` groups by HSN code and rate (the GSTR-1 HSN table shape), each with taxable value, CGST, SGST/UTGST, IGST and total. Sorted by rate, then HSN. | Module 02 #18; GSTR-1 table 12 |

All arithmetic is on integers. Products and quotients go through `BigInt` so even very large quantities can't lose precision, and the final paise must be safe integers.

## Assumptions (owner questions for sitting 2, except 3, now decided)

1. **Rates are not checked against a list.** The engine accepts any whole number of basis points from 0 to 10000, because GST rates change by notification (GST 2.0 in September 2025 moved most goods to 5% and 18%, with 40% for luxury and sin goods and special rates such as 3% for gold and 0.25% for rough diamonds). The item master (P1.7) checks the rate against the current list.
2. **No compensation cess.** Most cess ended with GST 2.0; whatever applies to tobacco is out of v1. Shops in scope sell such items at their printed MRP.
3. **Decided by the owner, 2026-11-18: a bill discount in rupees means the customer pays exactly that much less,** on any mix of tax-inclusive and tax-exclusive lines (rule 3; Module 02 Change log). *Was:* the rupee discount shared in each line's own price basis, which on a tax-exclusive bill made the customer pay the discount plus its tax less. Unchanged and still assumptions: the price basis is per line (`priceIncludesTax`), and a percent bill discount takes that percent off each line's own price.
4. **Exempt and 0% items on a regular business's bill** get 0 tax and appear under rate 0. The law lets one document serve as invoice and bill of supply together (Rule 46A); the printed layout is P1.15's job.
5. **Round-off is always on.** If the owner wants a setting to switch it off, it's one flag later.
6. **HSN codes:** 4, 6 or 8 digits on a tax invoice (at least 4 digits, Notification 78/2020-CT; 6 above ₹5 crore turnover), and optional on a bill of supply or plain bill. Module 01 #1 only lists the field, so the item master (P1.7) should use the same rule.

## Tests (Vitest, table-driven, test-first)

- `allocatePaise`: exact sum, proportional shares, largest-remainder ties go to the earlier line, zero weights get 0, a positive total with all-zero weights throws, negative or unsafe input throws.
- Line amount: whole and loose quantities (250 g of ₹42.50/kg = ₹10.63, half up), price 0, a quantity of 0 refused, a large quantity × large price stays exact.
- Discounts: item percent and amount, 100% discount, amount greater than the line refused; a rupee bill discount lowers the payable by exactly that amount on exclusive, inclusive, mixed and inter-state bills (₹10 off ₹100 + 18% → ₹108.00 = ₹91.52 + ₹8.24 + ₹8.24); a percent bill discount worked out once and shared; one-paisa bill discount over three lines.
- Exclusive and inclusive, intra- and inter-state, at 0%, 0.25%, 3%, 5%, 18% and 40%: worked examples checked by hand (₹118 inclusive at 18% = ₹100 + ₹9 + ₹9; 10% off it = ₹90 + ₹8.10 + ₹8.10).
- Odd paise: CGST always equals SGST; an inclusive line always totals exactly its net amount; IGST on the same line can differ by a paisa from CGST + SGST and that's expected; a pinned case where a half is 1 paisa under rate × taxable.
- UTGST names for the five Union Territories and Other Territory, SGST for Delhi, Puducherry and Jammu and Kashmir; no state tax name on IGST or composition bills; IGST when the states differ; codes outside the list refused.
- `INDIAN_STATES`: 37 entries, sorted, no repeated code or name, known codes and names, exactly six UTGST codes; `isStateCode` refuses 25, 28, 96, 99 and malformed codes.
- Composition and unregistered: no tax for inclusive or exclusive prices; round-off still applies; an empty HSN code accepted; selling to another state refused.
- Untyped input: `priceIncludesTax` as a string, number, null or missing refused; malformed or non-string HSN codes refused.
- Round-off: .49 down, .50 up, .00 none, totals under one rupee.
- Summaries: grouping by rate and by HSN + rate, sort order, totals equal to the sum of lines.
- Invariants over 400 generated carts (a seeded generator inside the test, no new dependency), with Union Territories, inter-state sales and percent and rupee bill discounts: a rupee bill discount lowers the payable by exactly that amount; totals (taxable, CGST, SGST, IGST, total, gross, subtotal, bill discount) equal the sums of the lines; tax-inclusive lines total their net amount exactly; CGST = SGST; each tax within 1 paisa of rate × taxable; taxable within 1 paisa of total ÷ (100% + rate); round-off within −49..+50; the rate and HSN summaries add up to every total; a state tax name only on CGST + SGST bills.
