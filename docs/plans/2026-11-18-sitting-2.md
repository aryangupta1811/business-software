## D4: entry log ID and time set by the database only

**Question:** should `entry_log`'s `id` and `created_at` always come from the database, so the app can't choose them? (PROGRESS P1.4 note (e).)

**Today:** the app's database role, `app_runtime`, can insert a log row with any `id` and any `created_at`. It can't change or delete a row afterwards (ADR 0028), but a bug, or anyone holding the app's database password, could write a row that looks as if it happened last month. Because the ID is a UUIDv7, which carries a time inside it, the ID can be backdated too, and the viewer orders by ID (P2.6).

**Recommended: yes.** Only the database sets both.

**Why:** the log's whole value is that nobody can fake it. "The app can't edit or delete history, and it can't backdate it either: the database stamps the time" is a stronger rule, and a stronger interview answer, than "the app can't edit or delete it".

**The price:** demo data (P1.20 (c)) is generated in one go, through the app's own services (ADR 0016). Bills can carry a business date from a clock the seed sets, but every log row will show the **day the seed ran**. So in the demo shop, months of bills have log rows all dated the same day, and a time-based flag like "late-night activity" (06 Q3) only fires on what visitors do live. The viewer can say so in one line ("demo history was generated on <date>").

**Options considered:**
- **A. Database only (recommended):** as above.
- **B. Keep today's rule:** the seed can backdate realistically, but so can a bug.
- **C. Database only, plus a separate seed-only role** that may set the time on demo databases: realistic history and strict rules for the app, but one more role and an exception to explain. The live demo is the only deployed database, so the exception would sit on the live server.

**Effect on P1.4 (Day 3), about an hour more:**
- A migration that removes `app_runtime`'s INSERT on the table and grants INSERT only on the other columns (column-level grants).
- A test that inserting `id` or `created_at` fails with `42501`, and a guard rule (`src/tenancy-guard.test.ts`) that `app_runtime` has no INSERT on those two columns.
- A dated note on ADR 0028, and P1.20 (c) updated.
- **Gotcha to handle:** Drizzle 0.45 names every column in an INSERT and sends `default` for the ones you leave out (checked in its `pg-core/dialect.js`). PostgreSQL checks the INSERT right for every column named, so after the grant change a plain Drizzle insert would probably be refused. `recordEntry` would then write its insert with an explicit column list. P1.4 proves this with the test first.

### 07 Q2: quiet hours

**Recommended:** yes. No WhatsApp messages from **22:00 to 08:00 IST** by default; the owner can change both times. Messages created during quiet hours wait in the outbox and go out at 08:00. In-app alerts are not affected.

**Why:** a shopkeeper's phone shouldn't buzz at night for things that can wait until morning, and nothing in v1 is urgent enough to break that.

**Effect:** small; a time check in the worker's sender (P2.8, Day 5), with tests around midnight and the edges.

### 07 Q3: which alerts can the owner turn off?

**Recommended:** every alert type, separately for the app and for WhatsApp. The defaults come from the business-type preset (`00` #1; medical starts with expiry alerts on).

**Why:** mixed shops differ (`00` #3); an alert the owner can't turn off becomes noise and gets ignored.

**Effect:** one settings section (P2.7 part 2, Day 4).

### 07 Q4: what time is the daily summary sent?

**Recommended:** at a time the owner sets, **21:30 IST** by default (before quiet hours start). It covers the day from midnight up to the moment it's sent.

**Why:** most shops close by then; one message a day is easy to read.

**Gap this raises, the server being off:** the demo server and database are stopped outside work sessions and every night at 23:30 IST (stage 5). A scheduled job can't run on a stopped server. **Recommended:** when the worker starts, it sends at most one missed summary (yesterday's), marked "late"; older missed ones are skipped. **Effect:** a catch-up check in P2.7/P2.8 with its own test.

### Claude's additions (proposed)

- **A1. WhatsApp sends digests only.** WhatsApp carries (1) the daily summary, (2) udhaar reminders the owner approves (`03` #15), and (3) bills and statements someone chooses to send (`02` #16, `03` #14). Low stock, expiry and flags go into the summary and the app, never one WhatsApp message per event. *Why:* every business-started message needs a Meta-approved template (ADR 0010), the test number is limited, a real shop would pay per message, and one message a day doesn't get ignored. *Effect:* keeps P2.8 small: two templates (summary, udhaar reminder).
- **A2. Raised once, cleared by itself.** A low-stock alert is raised when an item's stock at a branch falls to or below its low-stock level, and isn't raised again until stock goes back above it. It clears itself when restocked. Out of stock is the same at 0.
- **A3. Summary contents:** sales total and bill count, the split by payment mode (`02` #10), udhaar collected, items low or out of stock, batches expiring soon, flags from the entry log, who was absent. Per branch, with a total when there are several.
- **A4. Expiry window:** batches expiring within **30 days** by default, owner-settable; checked once a day.
- **A5. Where in-app alerts show:** the bell in the top bar, grouped by type with a count ("7 items low on stock"). Each person can dismiss an alert for themselves. Alerts belong to a branch; the owner sees all branches, staff see the branch they're logged into.
- **A6. Overdue udhaar:** shown in the summary by bucket (`03` #12); the reminder list itself stays the owner-approved flow in `03` #15.
- **A7. Language:** WhatsApp messages in the owner's chosen language (English, Hindi or Hinglish). Each language is a separate template for Meta to approve, so P2.8 starts with English and adds the others once approved; the Outbox page shows every language either way.

### 06 Q1: are the owner's own actions logged?

**Recommended:** yes. Close it as answered by `00` #20 ("every action is recorded against the person logged in"). The viewer can hide the owner's own entries with a filter.

**Why:** already decided; a log with gaps can't be trusted. **Effect:** none.

### 06 Q2: how long are logs kept?

**Recommended:** forever, as ADR 0028 has it today.

**Why:** GST law asks a business to keep its accounts for about six years (72 months after the annual return is due), and the log is the evidence behind them. Size isn't a problem: a busy shop writing 500 entries a day at roughly 1 KB each adds about 180 MB a year, against RDS's 20 GB.

**Effect:** none now. A future privacy request (India's DPDP Act) anonymises personal fields and keeps the records (ADR 0007, Consequences).

### 06 Q3: which patterns count as "unusual", and who sees the flags?

**Recommended:** **only the owner** sees flags: in the viewer, on the owner dashboard (`08`) and as a count in the daily summary (07 A3). Four rules in v1, each with a number the owner can change:

1. **Many cancellations:** one person cancels 3 or more bills in a day.
2. **Out-of-hours activity:** any change made outside shop hours (owner sets them; 08:00–22:00 by default).
3. **Big stock write-down:** one person's stock adjustments that reduce stock are worth more than ₹1,000 at purchase price in a day.
4. **Price changed back:** an item's price is lowered and raised back within the same day (the classic "cheap sale to a friend"). *Optional; adds about half a session to P2.6.*

**Why:** simple counts the owner understands beat clever rules nobody can explain. Flags are worked out from the log when they're shown, not stored, so changing a number re-checks old days too.

**Effect:** P2.6 (Day 5) includes rules 1–3, or 1–4; P2.9 (Day 7) shows the count on the dashboard. With D4 "yes", rule 2 only fires on live activity in the demo.

### 05 Q1: who marks attendance?

**Recommended:** **the owner**, or a staff member the owner switches "mark attendance" on for, on one daily register screen for the whole branch. Everyone starts as present; tap to change. Staff don't mark their own.

**Why:** the counter computer is shared, so self-marking is easy to fake; one person marking everyone takes under a minute. Changing a past day needs a reason and goes in the entry log.

**Effect:** P2.3 (Day 5) builds one register screen.

### 05 Q2: how are half-days, late arrivals and overtime counted in pay?

**Recommended:**
- **Monthly salary:** one day's pay = monthly salary ÷ the number of days in that month (28 to 31). Weekly offs, shop holidays and paid leaves are paid. An absent day takes off one day's pay, a half-day half of it.
- **Daily wage:** paid for each present day; a half-day pays half. Weekly offs and holidays are unpaid.
- **Late:** recorded and shown on the slip, **no automatic pay cut**. The owner can add a fine with a reason.
- **Overtime:** off by default; the owner switches it on per person with a rate in ₹ per hour. The app doesn't apply state labour-law rates.
- **Rounding:** worked out exactly in paise (ADR 0008); the net pay is rounded to the nearest rupee with a round-off line, like bills (`02` #6).

**Why:** dividing by the month's real days is the common practice and is easy to check by hand; no automatic cuts means no surprises for staff.

**Effect:** these are P2.4's business rules (Day 6), each with tests: months of 28, 29, 30 and 31 days, half-days, leave beyond the quota, joining or leaving mid-month, overtime on and off, round-off.

### 05 Q3: what if an advance is bigger than one month's salary?

**Recommended:** allowed. Each person has one running advance balance (every advance adds to it; recoveries reduce it), no interest. The owner sets a recovery amount per month, or "all at once". **A month's recovery never takes the pay below zero:** whatever doesn't fit carries to the next month by itself. The owner can change the instalment at any time. Only the owner gives advances.

**Why:** that's how shops really work, and it guarantees a slip never shows a negative salary. It is the owner's brief exactly: enter the advance, and the app works out what is still to be returned.

**Effect:** P2.4 rules and tests (carry-forward, several advances, a change of instalment). When someone leaves with an advance left, the balance stays visible until it's settled (paid back, or written off by the owner with a reason).

### 05 Q4: can a staff member work in more than one branch?

**Recommended:** close it as answered by `00` #35: one home branch for attendance and salary; the owner can let a login work in other branches. A day worked at another branch is marked present in the home branch's register.

**Effect:** none.

### 05 Q5: statutory deductions (PF, ESI): in scope or not?

**Recommended:** **out of v1**; noted in `ideas-backlog.md`.

**Why:** PF and ESI generally apply from 20 and 10 employees, which most shops this app is for don't reach, and their ceilings and rates change and would each need exact tests. The one-off deduction line (draft ideas) covers a shop that wants to show one.

**Effect:** keeps P2.4 to one session.

### Claude's additions (proposed)

- **S1. Salary month:** the calendar month (1st to last day), paid after it ends. Joining or leaving mid-month pays only the days between.
- **S2. A finalized month is locked.** Once a month's salary is finalized, its attendance can't change; a correction becomes an addition or deduction on the next month, with a reason. Same idea as "a saved bill is never edited" (`02` #14).
- **S3. Salary changes** (a raise) start from the 1st of a month; old rates are kept, never overwritten, so old slips stay right.
- **S4. Working on a weekly off or holiday** pays one extra day's pay (monthly staff) or the normal day's wage (daily staff).
- **S5. Who may do what:** setting salaries, giving advances and paying salaries are owner-only; marking attendance can be switched on for staff (Q1).
- **S6. Staff record fields:** name, phone, home branch, joining date, salary type and amount, weekly off day, paid leaves per month, overtime rate if any.
- **S7. Entry log:** marking or changing attendance (a change to a past day needs a reason), salary set or changed, advance given, recovery changed, salary paid, month finalized.
- **S8. Leaving:** deactivating a person (`00` #34) offers a final settlement slip: pay up to the last day, minus any advance left.
- **S9. The AI's "holidays" question** (`09`) counts absent days plus leave days; weekly offs and shop holidays are not "taken".
